    Setting up an agent against a local AI stack is config hell. Point Hermes at
        TabbyAPI, wire SearXNG for search, connect MCPO, pick a model, pray your keys
        survive. I've done it enough times to hate it — and once, my own tooling
        clobbered my Hermes install. So I built the thing I wished existed:
    
        **Basecamp — a portable Docker image (Hermes Agent + Ollama) that
        auto-discovers your entire local AI stack on first boot.**
    
        One command:
        bash
        docker pull jpanasuk/basecamp:1.0.2
        docker run -it --rm --network your-ai-network jpanasuk/basecamp:1.0.2
    
    
        What happens next:
        - Scans your network for Ollama, TabbyAPI, vLLM, LiteLLM, LocalAI,
          llama.cpp, SearXNG, MCPO, Open WebUI, SillyTavern
        - Connects Hermes to the best brain it finds (picks Ollama for the 64K
          context Hermes needs; offers TabbyAPI/vLLM interactively)
        - Generates a tavern CLI toolkit for the whole stack
        - Finds nothing? Falls back to its own bundled Ollama. Never stuck.
    
        The part I care most about — it cannot damage your Hermes install:
        - Config is env-scoped + a named provider, written only inside its own
          container home. Never touches your host's config.yaml or .env.
        - No secrets baked into the image. Keys entered at the connect screen,
          saved chmod 600 inside the volume.
    
        Tested the gauntlet way: pulled it fresh as a stranger, threw it at a live
        6-service stack, found and fixed 5 real bugs. It works — verified with real
        chats through the stack and its own fallback.
    
        Built on the Hermes team's excellent work — runs the official installer,
        ships all 71 bundled skills, stays out of your way.
